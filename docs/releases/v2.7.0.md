# Coordinator v2.7.0

This release adds queued competitions and day and night windows. Players enter without a seat count. When registration closes, the paid entries are split into as many even pools as needed, up to 25 players each, instead of the whole competition being refunded because it didn't fill ([#109](https://github.com/5day4cast/coordinator/pull/109)). It pins Keymeld v0.7.0, which adds key deposits.

**Changes**

- **Queued competitions.**
  - Creation: the admin form, `POST /api/v1/competitions/queued` and `admin competition create --queued`. Each takes the stations, window, entry fee and coordinator fee, a minimum pool size, a maximum of up to 25, and an optional entry cap (default 500).
  - At creation, the coordinator creates a reference oracle event with the competition's id. It freezes the lines players see and never takes entries.
  - Tickets are created on demand. Each buy-in is held in an Arkade escrow, as for single competitions.
  - When observation starts, the coordinator takes the first block at or after the close. The complete tickets (paid, entered, with a key deposit) are split into pools by a seed over the competition, those tickets and that block's hash. The coordinator cannot choose who plays whom. The shuffle decides only who shares a pool; inside a pool, the earliest entry still wins an exact tie.
  - Each pool is its own competition, with its own oracle event, Keymeld session and funding. It runs the usual Arkade lifecycle. Pool events copy the reference event's lines (NOAA Oracle 2.5.0).
  - If too few entries are complete for a pool, the queue is cancelled and every escrow is refunded to the player's Lightning Address. Paid tickets that no pool took are refunded the same way.
- **Consent to a template.** A queued entry's payout policy carries the competition's terms and the player's entry (`queued_entry`), not concrete contract terms, because the pool, its size, its oracle event and the player's slot exist only after kickoff.
  - The wallet checks the terms against the competition's oracle event and the oracle's key, which it fetches from the oracle itself, and against the entry form.
  - The entry key is sealed as a Keymeld deposit under the competition's terms.
  - There is still no extra screen: entering is consent, with one line saying where a refund goes.
- **Verifier.**
  - Before a pool's session admits a deposit, Keymeld's enclave recomputes the pool from its formation.
  - It derives each member's contract from the template and the oracle's signed statement of the pool's event (NOAA Oracle 2.4.0), and binds only that contract.
  - A single competition's entries cannot join a deposit session, and refund-only sessions cannot bind a contract.
- **Pages.** Queued competitions show how many players have entered, and that pools of up to 25 form at the start. After kickoff, the page links each pool, and a pool page links back.
- **Synth.** New scenarios: `queued_split`, `queued_one_pool`, `queued_too_few` and `queued_leftover_refund`.
- **Windows that score what they hold** ([#111](https://github.com/5day4cast/coordinator/pull/111)). A competition's window is a full day (24 hours or more, any start), a day (12:00–24:00 UTC) or a night (00:00–12:00 UTC).
  - A full day scores the high, the low and wind. A day scores the high and wind. A night scores the low and wind.
  - The admin form, the API and the CLI refuse any other window. The metrics are sent to the oracle, and picks on metrics the competition doesn't score are refused.
  - The entry form and leaderboard show only the metrics that are scored.
  - Queued competitions set their metrics the same way, and every pool inherits them.
- **Keymeld v0.7.0.** Every crate that names Keymeld pins the v0.7.0 release ([#110](https://github.com/5day4cast/coordinator/pull/110)).

**Required upgrade steps**

1. Deploy NOAA Oracle 2.5.0 first. It attests day and night windows, and queued competitions need its `lines_from_event`. The coordinator refuses a pool event whose lines differ from the reference event's, which is what an older oracle produces, so a queued competition fails safe and refunds.
2. Upgrade Keymeld to v0.7.0 and this release's verifier enclave together.
   - The gateway and enclaves exchange a binary message layout that gains new fields, so they must match.
   - Don't upgrade while an Arkade kickoff is inside its batch window.
   - If `crates/coordinator-wasm/keymeld-trusted-pcrs.json` lists measurements, add the v0.7.0 ones.
3. Deploy the coordinator and synth from `v2.7.0`. The migration `20261003000000_queued_competitions` adds the competition kind, pool and queue tables. Existing competitions become `single` and are unchanged.

**Compatibility**

- Single competitions, their policies, sessions and refunds behave as before.
  - Payout policies without a template serialize exactly as before.
  - Keymeld manifests and escrow commands without the new fields encode byte-identically.
- Rolling back to v2.6.0 is safe only when no queued competition is open. v2.6.0 cannot run a queued competition or its pools.

**Validation**

Rust tests cover:

- the statement encoding against the oracle's test vector;
- the template, its digest, and deriving each member's contract;
- the verifier's enrollment and binding checks for pools, including the refusals;
- creation, on-demand tickets, kickoff formation (deterministic and idempotent), too few players, and leftover refunds;
- the wallet's checks of a queued entry;
- an in-process run of a pool through two real Keymeld enclaves and the verifier: deposits, keygen, binding with the signed statement, and signing.
